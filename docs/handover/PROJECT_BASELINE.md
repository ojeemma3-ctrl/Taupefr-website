# TaupeFR — Original Product Baseline

## 1. Product summary

**TaupeFR** is an interactive digital platform concept designed to help international and external students moving to Bordeaux prepare for arrival and settle into student life.

The original product idea brings together practical information, guidance, relevant services and student-to-student support in one organised experience.

**Brand line:**  
“Your Home. Your Start. Your France.”

**Original product promise:**  
Help students find their way through the practical steps of moving to Bordeaux — from preparing accommodation and arrival to handling relevant administrative tasks and becoming part of the student community.

## 2. The problem being explored

Students moving to a new country and city may have to navigate multiple housing platforms, official websites, university information sources, local services and informal student networks.

The challenge is not necessarily that information does not exist. It can be difficult to identify:
- Which information is relevant to an individual student.
- Which action should be taken first.
- Which documents or conditions may be required.
- Which organisation or platform provides the actual service.
- How to prepare before arrival and continue after arrival.

These are problem hypotheses to validate with students, not claims that every international student has the same difficulties.

## 3. Core value proposition

TaupeFR aims to make the transition to Bordeaux easier by organising useful information and connecting students to existing services.

The guiding question in the original product experience is:

**“What should I do next?”**

The product should help students understand the next practical action and find the appropriate service, information or resource.

## 4. Target users

Primary audience:
- International students preparing to study in Bordeaux.
- Students arriving in Bordeaux from another country.
- External students who are unfamiliar with local housing, administrative and student-life systems.

Potential initial validation segment:
- First-time international degree-seeking students arriving from abroad.
- Students who do not yet have secured accommodation or local support.

Nigerian students may be a useful first interview group because they are accessible to the founder, but they should not automatically be treated as the final target market.

## 5. Original journey concept

The original MVP described a student journey broadly moving through:

**Admission → Accommodation → Arrival → Administration → Furniture / Marketplace → Community → Settled**

This is a product-organising journey, not a claim that every student must follow a rigid sequence. Students may have already completed some steps or may need them in a different order.

## 6. Original MVP modules

- Landing / welcome
- Student journey
- Student profile
- Personalised dashboard
- Housing guidance and links
- Arrival information
- Administrative guidance
- Student marketplace
- Community support
- Settled / ongoing student-life information
- Reusable Taupe Assistant

## 7. What TaupeFR is — and is not

TaupeFR is:
- A guidance and information-orchestration layer.
- A way to organise fragmented information and useful services.
- A platform that directs students to relevant existing providers.
- A student journey and support experience.

TaupeFR is not:
- A landlord or housing provider.
- A guarantor.
- A guarantee that a student will obtain accommodation.
- A guarantee of visa, CAF, residence-permit, bank or other administrative approval.
- A replacement for CROUS, CAF, Campus France, universities, prefectures, housing platforms or other official providers.
- A reason to duplicate every service that already exists.

## 8. Product principles

1. Make the next action understandable.
2. Explain why a task matters.
3. Tell the student where the actual task can be completed.
4. Use plain, accessible language.
5. Distinguish TaupeFR guidance from official information.
6. Link to authoritative sources for official procedures.
7. Avoid promising eligibility or outcomes.
8. Do not show a procedure as universally required when it depends on the student's situation.
9. Keep the product useful before and after arrival.
10. Make the prototype demonstrate a coherent student experience rather than a collection of unrelated pages.

## 9. Validation questions

The product still needs evidence about:
- Which student groups experience the greatest difficulty.
- Which parts of the journey create the most confusion.
- Which information students currently use and trust.
- Where existing services are difficult to navigate.
- Whether students would use a single guidance layer.
- Whether universities, student organisations or service providers would distribute or support it.
- Whether students or partners would pay for any part of the service.

Do not turn assumptions into market facts without evidence.
