# TaupeFR — Product Boundaries and Business Decisions

## 1. Product role

TaupeFR is a guidance and orchestration layer across existing information and services.

It helps a student understand:
- What may apply to their situation.
- What action to consider next.
- What information or documents to prepare.
- Where the real service or process is provided.

## 2. Housing model

TaupeFR should not present itself as the owner or provider of housing.

Potential MVP approach:
- Direct students to existing housing platforms and providers.
- Provide practical search guidance and scam-awareness information.
- Explore trusted partners and referrals.
- Use approved feeds/APIs or formal partnerships if listings are ever displayed inside TaupeFR.
- Do not scrape housing platforms or republish their listings without permission.

TaupeFR is not a guarantor and does not guarantee accommodation.

## 3. Administration model

For administrative procedures:
- Explain the process in plain language.
- Identify who the procedure may apply to.
- Link to the official organisation.
- Make clear that official providers determine requirements, eligibility and outcomes.
- Keep information date-aware and review it regularly.

Never state that a permanent address is universally required for every bank, SIM, CAF, health-insurance, visa or residence-permit process. Requirements can vary; temporary accommodation or hosted arrangements may be accepted in some contexts.

Do not promise that a student qualifies for CAF, healthcare, a residence permit or any other public service.

## 4. Marketplace model

The original concept explored a student-to-student marketplace:
- Furniture.
- Kitchenware.
- Bicycles.
- Appliances.
- Other useful student essentials.

A possible initial supply approach is to seed listings through outgoing students. This is a concept to validate, not an existing operational network.

Any transaction, payment, moderation, safety and dispute-handling functions would need their own design and operational plan before launch.

## 5. Community model

Community features may help students discover:
- Peer support.
- Student groups.
- Local activities.
- Relevant university or student associations.

Do not display invented real members, active groups or verified partnerships.

## 6. Business model hypotheses

Potential revenue models explored:
- Referral or partnership revenue from relevant service providers.
- Housing-related partner or referral arrangements.
- Marketplace transaction fees.
- University or institutional partnerships.
- Freemium student access and possible premium services.

These are hypotheses for validation. Do not describe them as secured revenue, signed partnerships or proven willingness to pay.

## 7. Partner and source principles

Potential organisations and sources may include:
- CROUS.
- CAF.
- Campus France.
- Universities and international welcome services.
- Housing platforms.
- Student associations and buddy programmes.
- Local service providers.

They are potential sources, channels or partners, not confirmed partners unless a real agreement exists.

## 8. Trust and safety

- Clearly identify official sources.
- Do not impersonate government or university services.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
- Do not expose sensitive student documents in a demo.
- Do not promise results.
- Explain that users should confirm changing requirements with the responsible organisation.
